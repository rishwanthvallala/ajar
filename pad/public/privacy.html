Privacy

Effective 5 October 2026

pad is a code scratchpad at code.rishwanth.dev: open it, type, run, and send the link. It is a personal project run by Rishwanth Vallala. Questions, or a request to see or delete your data: vallalarishwanth@gmail.com.

In short.

  • You can use pad without an account. Signing in is only for pads you control.
  • Signing in gives us your name, email address and an account id from Google or GitHub — nothing else.
  • Files you put in a pad are stored on our server, unencrypted.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, no selling or sharing your data, no email.

If you sign in

You can sign in with Google or GitHub. We never see or store a password.

  • From Google we receive your Google account id, your email address and your name (the openid, email and profile permissions).
  • From GitHub we receive your GitHub account id, your username, and your name and email address if you have made them public (the read:user permission).

We store which service you signed in with, that account id, your email address, your name, when you first signed in, and when each sign-in began. They are used only to recognise you when you come back, to show you whose account you are in, and in the operator's view described below; an email address is also how the operator is recognised as the operator. The access the service grants is used once, during sign-in, to read those details, and is not kept. We cannot see your contacts, files, repositories, or anything else in your Google or GitHub account.

Cookies

  • Once you sign in, a cookie holds a random token that keeps you signed in for 30 days, or until you sign out. The server keeps only a one-way hash of it.
  • While a sign-in is in progress, a second cookie lasts at most 10 minutes, to make sure the sign-in finishes in the browser that started it.

There are no other cookies, and none from anyone else.

Your pads

  • The files in a pad are stored on our server so that whoever has the link can open them. They are not encrypted, and the person who runs pad can read them. Do not put passwords, keys or anything sensitive in a pad.
  • A pad anyone can open can be read and changed by anyone with its link, and is deleted after 90 days in which nobody opens it.
  • A pad you own is kept until you delete it, and who can view or edit it is your choice. Deleting it from Your pads deletes its files straight away.
  • The links you share carry a code after the #. Browsers never send that part to a server, so it does not appear in our logs. We keep each code only as a one-way hash, plus an encrypted copy so that you can copy the link again from Your pads.

When you run code

Code runs inside your own browser, not on our server. To do that, your browser downloads the runtime — most of it from us, and some from Wasmer (registry.wasmer.io and cdn.wasmer.io), which sees your IP address as any site you download from does. When code in a pad installs a Python package, the connection goes through our server to the Python Package Index (pypi.org and files.pythonhosted.org), and nowhere else; its name lookups go through our server to Cloudflare's DNS service. The connection is encrypted end to end, so we see which of those hosts it reached, not what was sent.

Logs

Our web server records each request — the IP address it came from, the address requested, the time, and the browser's description of itself — to keep the service secure, to limit abuse, and to fix problems. Older entries are deleted automatically as the logs fill. IP addresses are also held in memory for up to a day to apply rate limits.

On your device

Your browser keeps your settings — theme, layout, colours — and the codes of pad links you have opened, in its own storage on your device. They stay there until you clear your browser's data; signing out, or deleting your account, forgets the link codes.

What the person who runs pad sees

To run the service, its operator has a page only they can open, built from what is described above — nothing extra is collected for it. It shows totals: how many accounts and pads there are, how many people are in a pad right now, how much is stored, and sign-ups and new pads per day. And it lists accounts, with each one's name, email address, the service they signed in with, when they joined and last signed in, and how many pads and how much space they use; and the ten largest pads, by name, with their size and when each was last opened — for keeping the service within its limits and dealing with abuse.

Who else is involved

pad runs on Amazon Web Services, in its Mumbai region, which stores and processes the data above on our behalf. We do not sell, rent or share your data with anyone else, and do not use it for advertising. We would disclose it only if the law required us to.

Google user data

pad's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The information pad receives from Google — your account id, email address and name — is used only to sign you in, to show you which account you are using, and in the operator's view described above. It is not shared, sold, used for advertising, or used to train any model.

Your choices

  • Delete any pad you own from Your pads; its files go immediately.
  • Sign out from Your pads at any time.
  • Delete your account from the foot of Your pads: your name, email address and sign-ins are removed and every pad you own is deleted, straight away. Signing in again later starts a new, empty account.
  • To get a copy of what is stored about you, or to have your account deleted if you can no longer sign in, email the address above; it will be done within 30 days.
  • You can also withdraw pad's access from your Google account or under GitHub's Authorized OAuth Apps.

Children

pad is not meant for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If what pad collects or how it uses it changes, this page will change first, with a new date at the top.
